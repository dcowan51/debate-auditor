## SECTION B — Schopenhauer Stratagem Analysis

### Stratagem 1 — Extension (HIGH SEVERITY)

**Timestamp:** 1:17

**Quote:** *"If that were true, passages like Isaiah 44:24 should clearly say, 'We are the Lord. We stretched out the heavens alone who spread abroad the earth by ourselves.' The fact it doesn't say this is proof positive."*

The speaker extends the Trinitarian position beyond what it claims. Trinitarianism does not teach that every divine speech act must deploy plural pronouns; it teaches that three Persons share one divine essence and act as a single unified subject. The speaker demands that Trinitarian theology require plural speech forms as a necessary consequence — a requirement no Trinitarian theologian has ever endorsed. This extension fabricates a criterion for falsification that the actual position does not imply, making it structurally unfalsifiable to the speaker's advantage.

### Stratagem 2 — Equivocation (MEDIUM SEVERITY)

**Timestamp:** 0:44 / 1:07

**Quote:** *"who stretches out the heavens alone, who spreads out the earth by myself"* / *"they could actually never be alone"*

The speaker exploits the ambiguity of "alone" (Hebrew *לְבַדִּי*, by myself/alone). In Isaiah 44:24, "alone" most naturally means without the help of any other god or created agent — a monotheistic declaration against Babylonian polytheism. The speaker treats it as necessarily implying singularity of person. These are not the same claim. Trinitarians read "alone" as excluding other divine beings, not as excluding intra-divine persons within the one God. The equivocation is load-bearing: the entire Isaiah 44:24 argument collapses if the two senses of "alone" are distinguished.

### Stratagem 11 — Induction with Concealed Counterexamples (HIGH SEVERITY)

**Timestamp:** 0:14 / 1:39

**Quote:** *"The amount of times it doesn't can be counted on one hand."*

The speaker claims plural divine references are so rare they barely exist, then acknowledges "let us make man in our image" (Gen 1:26). In fact, the plural forms include: Gen 1:26 ("let us make… our image"), Gen 3:22 ("man has become like one of us"), Gen 11:7 ("come, let us go down"), Isaiah 6:8 ("who will go for us?"), along with the entire divine council tradition across the Psalms (Ps 82, Ps 89:7). The claim suppresses documented counterexamples while asserting they scarcely exist. This is the same stratagem identified in Session 1 (re: John 1:18).

### Stratagem 12 — Pre-loading Advantageous Terms (HIGH SEVERITY)

The speaker presents this re-definition as if it were the natural reading, without acknowledging that the overwhelming consensus in NT scholarship treats the Johannine Logos as a personal, pre-existent agent drawing on the hypostatized Wisdom tradition (Prov 8, Wis 7, Sir 24).

**2. "There is no evidence in linguistics that you use singular pronouns to refer to a plurality of persons"**

**Rating:** Likely False — directly contradicted by standard Hebrew grammar references. Confidence: High.
**Evidence:** GKC §124g documents the *pluralis majestatis* in Hebrew; Joüon-Muraoka §136d discusses plural of excellence; the *plural of deliberation* is also recognized as a distinct phenomenon in Semitic studies. All are well-attested in reference grammars.
**What would settle it:** Engagement with GKC or Joüon-Muraoka on *pluralis majestatis*; any standard Hebrew grammar would suffice.
**Timestamp + Quote:** 2:24 — *"There is no evidence in linguistics that you use singular pronouns to refer to a plurality of persons."*

---

**3. Trinitarian use of collective singular (all persons referred to as one) necessarily entails partialism**

**Rating:** Misleading — misrepresents the Trinitarian response. Confidence: High.
**Evidence:** Trinitarian theology holds that singular pronouns apply to God because the three Persons share one undivided divine essence (*ousia*) — not because each Person is a "part" of God. On Trinitarian grounds, the oneness is prior to the threeness. Partialism is the view that each Person is one-third of God; this is explicitly condemned. The speaker's argument only lands if Trinitarians are committed to a social-trinity model, which classical Trinitarianism rejects. — [https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/15047a.htm](https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/15047a.htm)
**What would settle it:** A Trinitarian theologian's stated account of why singular language is appropriate within their framework.
**Timestamp + Quote:** 3:05 — *"you can't actually appeal to the persons as a whole without entailing partialism"*

---

**4. "Through" (*dia* + genitive) in NT creation texts rules out personal agency, indicating only impersonal instrumental causation**

**Rating:** Misleading — overstates a contested grammatical claim. Confidence: Medium-High.
**Evidence:** *Dia* + genitive does mark instrumental agency in NT Greek, but this does not require impersonal agency. The same construction in Heb 1:2 ("through whom he also created the worlds") appears within a passage that identifies the Son as the radiance of God's glory, the exact imprint of his nature, upholding all things by his powerful word — all highly personal language. Standard NT Greek grammars (Wallace, *Greek Grammar Beyond the Basics*, pp. 368–369) treat instrumental *dia* as compatible with personal agents. — Daniel B. Wallace, *Greek Grammar Beyond the Basics* (Zondervan, 1996).
**What would settle it:** A survey of *dia* + genitive constructions where the referent is unambiguously personal vs. impersonal, compared to the creation texts.
**Timestamp + Quote:** 4:40 — *"every single time you see the word through is literally a genative of instrument and agency"*

---

**5. NT creation passages "are not saying that Jesus was a conscious person who created the world with the father"**

**Rating:** Misleading — contradicts the mainstream exegetical consensus on these texts. Confidence: Medium-High.
**Evidence:** Col 1:15–20 sustains personal language throughout (*ἐν αὐτῷ*, *αὐτός ἐστιν πρὸ πάντων*). John 1:1–3 identifies the *Logos* as both *with* God (πρὸς τὸν θεόν — relational personal preposition) and as God, prior to creation. The impersonal-word reading requires systematic reinterpretation against the grain of the syntax. — Bauckham, *God Crucified* (Eerdmans, 1998); Lincoln, *The Gospel According to Saint John* (BNTC, 2005).
**What would settle it:** A full syntactical analysis of John 1:1–3 and Col 1:15–20 in dialogue with critical commentaries.
**Timestamp + Quote:** 5:08–5:19 — *"these passages are not saying that Jesus was a conscious person who created the world with the father… No, they're saying that what pre-existed is God's word"*

**6. "They knew the Messiah would be a human raised up from among them from the line of David, not this preexistent spirit being"**

**Rating:** Misleading — oversimplifies diverse and contested Second Temple messianic expectations. Confidence: Medium.
**Evidence:** While Davidic messianism is the dominant strand, Second Temple literature attests pre-existence traditions: the "Son of Man" figure in 1 Enoch 46–48 is described as named before creation; 4 Ezra 12–13 reflects a pre-existent heavenly figure; the Qumran *Melchizedek* text (11Q13) presents a heavenly redeemer. Pre-existence was not foreign to first-century Jewish messianic thinking. — John J. Collins, *The Scepter and the Star* (Eerdmans, 2010).
**What would settle it:** A systematic survey of Second Temple messianic texts addressing the presence or absence of pre-existence traditions.
**Timestamp + Quote:** 5:27–5:33 — *"they knew the Messiah would be a human raised up from among them from the line of David, not this preexistent spirit being"*

**7. "The Bible over 7,000 times confirms that God is one person"**

**Rating:** Misleading — conflates grammatical singularity with the metaphysical claim of personal singularity. Confidence: High.
**Evidence:** Singular grammatical forms confirm monotheism — that there is one God — but do not settle the question of whether that one God is a single person or a tri-personal being. Classical Trinitarian theology affirms monotheism precisely on the basis of these texts; the speaker treats agreement on the data as agreement on the metaphysical conclusion, which begs the question.

**Timestamp + Quote:** 5:46–5:53 — *"the Bible over 7,000 times confirms that God is one person who created the world alone"*
