## SECTION B — Schopenhauer Stratagem Analysis

### Stratagem 1 — The Extension (Generalization)

**Timestamp:** p. 5
**Quote:** **"If you see an apostle speaking things contrary to what the Master Himself said, then you know that person is not a true apostle but a pretender, a traitor, and a false apostle who was never chosen by the Master, but was selected by the adversary, masquerading as the Master."**
**Analysis:** The author extends the reasonable principle that an emissary should be consistent with the one who sent him into an absolute binary: any apparent disagreement = false apostle chosen by satan. This extension eliminates the possibility of contextual application, progressive revelation, or legitimate interpretive development — all of which are normal in ancient religious traditions. It sets up an unfalsifiable framework where only texts that agree with the author's reading of Jesus's words are "authentic."

### Stratagem 6 — Concealed Petitio Principii (Begging the Question)

**Timestamp:** pp. 4–5
**Quote:** **"Most certainly I tell you, that the Master Yeshua did not instruct His disciples in ignorance, neither did He choose His apostles at random... He certainly did not choose twelve 'inferior' apostles so that one greater and more knowledgeable than them could appear later, in order to teach things contrary to what He Himself said."**
**Analysis:** The entire argument assumes its own conclusion. The author presupposes that Paul teaches "things contrary to what [Jesus] Himself said," then constructs a theological framework (apostles cannot contradict Jesus → Paul contradicts Jesus → Paul is false) that only works if the premise is already accepted. The reader is never shown specific contradictions; they are simply assumed. This is textbook petitio principii.

### Stratagem 11 — Induction with Concealed Counterexamples

**Timestamp:** p. 5
**Quote:** **"For the Master did not come to bring some pretended 'new dispensation.' Certainly not an alleged 'dispensation of grace,' because the Scriptures say that Noah found Grace in the eyes of the Most High."**
**Analysis:** The author cites Genesis 6:8 (Noah found grace) and Exodus 20:6 (grace to those who love Him) to argue grace is not new. This is a fair observation in isolation. However, it conceals the counterexample that "dispensationalism" as a theological system is not merely about the existence of grace — it concerns the *administration* of God's relationship with humanity across different periods. The author attacks a strawman version of dispensationalism (that grace didn't exist before Christ) rather than the actual theological framework. Furthermore, texts like Jeremiah 31:31-34 ("I will make a new covenant... not like the covenant I made with their fathers") are not addressed.

### Stratagem 16 — Argumentum ad Hominem (Attack the Source)

**Timestamp:** p. 6
**Quote:** **"Little children do not accept the words of a man simply because he was put into a collection of writings compiled hundreds of years ago. If the Master wanted us to accept everyone claiming to be an apostle He would not have warned us that false apostles would come."**
**Analysis:** Rather than engaging with Pauline theology on its merits — examining specific claims, comparing them to Jesus's teachings point by point — the author attacks Paul's credibility by categorizing him as "a man... put into a collection of writings compiled hundreds of years ago." This shifts the argument from content to source, bypassing the need to actually demonstrate contradictions.

### Stratagem 30 — Argumentum ad Verecundiam (Appeal to Authority — Selective)

**Timestamp:** p. 8, Table of Contents
**Quote:** **"I have assembled the following documents from the hands of the Apostles chosen by our Master and I'm giving them to you to guide you back into the true faith."**
**Analysis:** The author presents the Clementine literature (3rd–4th century pseudepigraphical works) and his own "Reconstructed Gospel of the Hebrews" as documents "from the hands of the Apostles." This is a selective appeal to authority: he rejects the canonical process that included Paul's letters while simultaneously claiming his own curated collection represents authentic apostolic authorship — despite the fact that the Clementine literature is universally recognized by scholars as pseudonymous and centuries later than the apostolic period.

### Stratagem 32 — Dismissal by Categorization

**Timestamp:** pp. 5–6
**Quote:** **"So when you hear men speaking pompous words which they themselves created that the Master Himself never uttered: words like 'dispensationalism' to promote an ideology that contradicts and seeks to invalidate the plain words of our Savior, you should know that they are trying to lead you astray."**
**Analysis:** By categorizing dispensationalist language as "pompous words which they themselves created," the author dismisses an entire theological tradition (with substantial scholarly literature spanning centuries) without engaging any of its actual arguments. The word "dispensation" (Greek: oikonomia) does in fact appear in the Greek New Testament (Eph 1:10, 3:2, Col 1:25), though these are Pauline texts the author has already rejected.

### Weasel-Word Flags

1. **"Little children"** — Used repeatedly (pp. 5, 6, 7, 8) as a pastoral authority device. While drawn from Johannine vocabulary (1 John 2:1), its repeated use positions the author as a spiritual father whose audience should receive his claims with childlike trust rather than critical examination.

2. **"those who tell you"** — Used on p. 6: "do not believe those who tell you that Yeshua changed His Father's word." This unnamed "those" creates an implied enemy without identifying who specifically teaches this or what their actual argument is, making the claim impossible to verify or contest.

## SECTION F — Most Likely False or Misleading Claims

### 1. The "Reconstructed Gospel of the Hebrews" represents the original New Covenant scripture.
**Rating:** Likely false — High confidence
**Reason:** The Gospel of the Hebrews is a lost text known only from patristic fragments. The author's "reconstruction" is an editorial compilation blending canonical Matthew with fragments from Jerome, Origen, Epiphanius, and other church fathers, plus material from the Protevangelium of James. This is a modern interpretive project, not a recovery of an ancient manuscript.
**Evidence:** Eusebius (Church History 3.25.5) distinguishes the Gospel of the Hebrews from canonical Matthew. Modern scholarship (Kloppenborg, Vielhauer, Klijn) identifies at least three distinct "Jewish-Christian Gospels" conflated in patristic testimony. See: [Hebrew Gospel hypothesis - Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_Gospel_hypothesis)
**What would settle it:** Discovery of a complete manuscript of the Gospel of the Hebrews for comparison.
**Quote:** **"The Reconstructed Gospel Of The Hebrews"** — Title, p. 9

### 2. The Clementine literature (Clement's Letter to James, Sermons of Peter) are authentic apostolic documents.
**Rating:** Likely false — High confidence
**Reason:** The Clementine Homilies and Recognitions are universally dated by scholars to the 3rd–4th century CE. They are pseudepigraphical (falsely attributed to Clement of Rome). Including them as "documents from the hands of the Apostles" is a core historical error.
**Evidence:** [Clementine literature - Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clementine_literature); [NASSCAL Pseudo-Clementines entry](https://www.nasscal.com/e-clavis-christian-apocrypha/pseudo-clementines/). Scholarly consensus: common source (Periodoi Petrou) dates to early 3rd century; final redactions to late 3rd–early 4th century.
**What would settle it:** Manuscript evidence dating any Clementine text to the 1st century.
**Quote:** **"I have assembled the following documents from the hands of the Apostles chosen by our Master"** — p. 8

### 3. "Yahweh" is properly pronounced "Yah-wuh."
**Rating:** Misleading — Medium confidence
**Reason:** The pronunciation of the Tetragrammaton is debated. The most common scholarly reconstructions are "Yahweh" (two syllables, yah-WEH) based on Theodoret's testimony and Greek transliterations (Ιαβε). "Yah-wuh" is a minority pronunciation not supported by the primary evidence.
**Evidence:** Frank Cross, *Canaanite Myth and Hebrew Epic* (1973); the Murasu texts; Greek transcription evidence consistently points to a final stressed syllable (-weh), not an unstressed (-wuh).
**What would settle it:** Discovery of a vocalized ancient text containing the full name.
**Quote:** **"'Yahweh' is properly pronounced 'Yah-wuh'"** — p. 3

### 4. Paul is implicitly a false apostle selected by the adversary.
**Rating:** Misleading — High confidence
**Reason:** Though never named, the entire letter builds a framework to reject Paul: apostles cannot contradict Jesus → someone in the NT contradicts Jesus → that person is a false apostle chosen by "the adversary." This is misleading because: (a) the author never demonstrates specific contradictions between Paul and Jesus; (b) Paul's apostleship was acknowledged by the Jerusalem pillars including James and Peter (Galatians 2:9); (c) Acts — which the author appears to accept in modified form ("NazActs") — records Paul's commission; and (d) 2 Peter 3:15-16, if accepted as authentic, refers to Paul's letters as scripture.
**Evidence:** Galatians 2:7-9 (James, Cephas, and John gave Paul the right hand of fellowship); Acts 15 (Jerusalem Council affirms Paul's Gentile mission).
**What would settle it:** The author providing specific, documented contradictions between Jesus's recorded words and Paul's teachings, with contextual analysis.
**Quote:** **"if you see an apostle speaking things contrary to what the Master Himself said, then you know that person is not a true apostle but a pretender, a traitor, and a false apostle"** — p. 5

### 5. Matthew 24:11 and 24:24 ("false prophets/apostles") refer to Paul or Pauline teachers.
**Rating:** Misleading — High confidence
**Reason:** The Matthean context is the Olivet Discourse, a prophecy about end-times tribulation. "False prophets" (pseudoprophetai) in this passage refers to eschatological deceivers during the great tribulation, not to fellow apostles within the early church. Applying this to Paul requires importing a meaning foreign to the text's literary and historical context.
**Evidence:** W.D. Davies and Dale Allison, *Matthew* (ICC Commentary), vol. 3, on Matt 24:11; R.T. France, *The Gospel of Matthew* (NICNT) on the Olivet Discourse context.
**What would settle it:** Any ancient source applying Matt 24:11 specifically to Paul.
**Quote:** The author cites **"Matthew 24:11 & 24"** as proof the Master "warned us that false apostles would come" — p. 6

### 6. Today, Esau is "masquerading as Jacob" and modern nations fulfilling Ezekiel 36:5 represent Edom.
**Rating:** Likely false — Medium confidence
**Reason:** This claim enters the territory of racialized biblical interpretation (identifying modern nations/peoples with Esau/Edom). There is no scholarly basis for identifying any modern nation-state or ethnic group with biblical Esau/Edom. The prophetic texts about Edom (Malachi 1:2-5, Obadiah, Ezekiel 35-36) refer to the historical Edomite kingdom, which ceased to exist as a distinct polity in the late antiquity period (absorbed by the Nabataeans, then Rome). Applying these texts to modern geopolitics is eisegesis.
**Evidence:** John R. Bartlett, *Edom and the Edomites* (JSOT Supplement, 1989); the historical Edomites were absorbed into Idumean populations by the Hellenistic period.
**What would settle it:** Archaeological or genetic evidence linking a modern population to ancient Edom in the way the author implies.
**Quote:** **"today Esau is masquerading as Jacob to secure the blessing of the nations"** — p. 8

### 7. Esau is "the only people in all of Scripture whom God hates."
**Rating:** Misleading — High confidence
**Reason:** Malachi 1:2-3 does say "Esau I have hated," but the author's claim that Esau is "the only people in all of Scripture whom God hates" is an overstatement. The Hebrew Bible contains expressions of divine anger and rejection toward multiple nations and peoples (e.g., the Amalekites in 1 Samuel 15:2-3; the inhabitants of Canaan in Deuteronomy 7:1-2). The word "hate" (Hebrew: שָׂנֵא, sane) in Malachi is widely understood by scholars as a comparative idiom meaning "loved less" or "not chosen" (cf. Luke 14:26 where "hate" means "love less"). Presenting this as unique, literal hatred distorts the text.
**Evidence:** Beth Glazier-McDonald, *Malachi: The Divine Messenger* (SBL Dissertation Series, 1987); the comparative use of "hate" in Semitic idiom.
**What would settle it:** A comprehensive survey of all divine "hatred" language in the Hebrew Bible.
**Quote:** **"the only people in all of Scripture whom God hates"** — p. 8

### 8. Written Scripture transmitted by human hands is not the unadulterated Word of God; only the Holy Spirit speaking directly constitutes the true Word.
**Rating:** Misleading — Medium confidence
**Reason:** This claim is internally contradictory within the author's own project. The author simultaneously (a) argues that written scripture is unreliable because it passes through human hands, yet (b) publishes a 774-page book of written scriptures that he compiled, translated, and edited, asking readers to use these texts "as a tool to find your way back to the Truth." If written texts are unreliable vessels for God's Word, the author's own compilation is subject to the same limitation. The author partially acknowledges this ("still reliable and profitable but not infallible"), but the strong rhetoric against written scripture undermines the very book he is presenting.
**Evidence:** Internal contradiction within the document itself.
**What would settle it:** The author clarifying which written texts he considers authoritative and why his editorial choices are more reliable than the canonical process he rejects.
**Quote:** **"if that human being writes that message on paper and gives it to you it is no longer the unadulterated, unmitigated Word of God but it is rather man's corrupted understanding"** — p. 8

## SECTION H — Mostly True & Fair Claims

1. **The Sacred Name (YHWH) appears in Paleo Hebrew script in the Dead Sea Scrolls** — True. Several DSS manuscripts (e.g., 4QLXXLevb, 8HevXIIgr) preserve the Tetragrammaton in Paleo Hebrew within otherwise square-script or Greek texts. Confirmed by Emanuel Tov, *Scribal Practices and Approaches Reflected in the Texts Found in the Judean Desert* (2004).

2. **Yeshua had foreknowledge (citing Matt 26:10, John 16:30)** — These verses do attribute supernatural knowledge to Jesus. Matthew 26:10: "Jesus, aware of this, said to them..." John 16:30: "Now we know that you know all things." Accurate citation.

3. **2 Peter 2:1-3 warns of false teachers who will exploit with fabricated stories** — Accurate citation. The NRSVUE reads: "there will be false teachers among you, who will secretly bring in destructive opinions... and in their greed they will exploit you with deceptive words."

4. **The term "dispensationalism" does not appear in Jesus's recorded words** — True. The systematic theology known as "dispensationalism" was developed by John Nelson Darby in the 1830s. The word itself does not appear in any gospel text.

5. **Genesis 6:8 states Noah found grace (Hebrew: חֵן, chen) in the eyes of YHWH** — True. Accurate citation.

6. **Exodus 20:6 describes God showing steadfast love (Hebrew: חֶסֶד, hesed) to those who love Him and keep His commandments** — True. Accurate citation, though the Hebrew word is chesed (covenant loyalty/lovingkindness) rather than chen (grace/favor). The theological point that God's favor existed before the New Covenant is sound.

7. **Matthew 5:18 records Jesus saying not one jot or tittle will pass from the Law** — True. Accurate citation. NRSVUE: "until heaven and earth pass away, not one letter, not one stroke of a letter, will pass from the law until all is accomplished."

8. **Matthew 24:35 records Jesus saying "Heaven and earth will pass away, but my words will not pass away"** — True. Accurate citation.

9. **John 16:13 records Jesus promising the Spirit of Truth will guide into all truth** — True. Accurate citation.

10. **Isaiah 59:21 contains a covenant promise that God's Spirit and words will not depart** — True. Accurate citation of the verse's content, though the author's application (universalized to mean the Spirit can never be removed from anyone) goes beyond the verse's covenantal context.

11. **1 Peter 2:9-10 describes believers as "a chosen race, a royal priesthood"** — True. Accurate citation.

12. **John 5:39-40 records Jesus saying "You search the scriptures because you think that in them you have eternal life... yet you refuse to come to me"** — True. Accurate citation.

13. **The Didache is an early Christian text** — True. Widely dated to the late 1st or early 2nd century, making it among the earliest non-canonical Christian writings. Its inclusion in the collection is a defensible editorial choice.

14. **The Gospel of the Hebrews baptism account includes the phrase "my mother the Holy Spirit"** — True. This fragment is preserved by Origen (*Commentary on John* 2.12.87) and Jerome (*Commentary on Isaiah* 40.9). The author accurately incorporates a known patristic fragment into his reconstructed text.
