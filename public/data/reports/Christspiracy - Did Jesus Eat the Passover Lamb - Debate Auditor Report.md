## SOURCE CONTEXT VERIFICATION

### 1. Full-Chapter Context Check: Jeremiah 7

The speaker quotes Jeremiah 7:22 in isolation. The full chapter reveals:

- **7:9** — "Will you steal, murder, commit adultery, swear falsely, make offerings to Baal, and go after other gods that you have not known…?"
- **7:18** — "The children gather wood, the fathers kindle fire, and the women knead dough, to make cakes for the queen of heaven."
- **7:31** — "And they go on building the high place of Topheth… to burn their sons and their daughters in the fire."

The chapter is a polemic against syncretistic worship — practicing idolatry while relying on the temple as a talisman. Verse 22 functions within this context as a rhetorical device (dialectical negation): "I did not primarily command sacrifice; I commanded obedience." This is standard prophetic rhetoric (cf. Isaiah 1:11–17, Amos 5:21–25, Micah 6:6–8), all of which critique the *attitude* behind sacrifice, not the institution itself. Presenting this verse as evidence that God never commanded sacrifice is a severe decontextualization, especially given that Exodus 12, Leviticus 1–7, and Deuteronomy 16 — all from the same canonical corpus — contain detailed sacrificial legislation.

**Verdict:** Core decontextualization. Scored as Misleading.

### 2. Same-Source Cross-Reference: The Gospels

The speaker appeals to the gospels as evidence that no lamb was present at the Last Supper. The same gospels also contain:

- **Luke 22:7** — "Then came the day of Unleavened Bread, on which the Passover lamb had to be sacrificed."
- **Mark 14:12** — "On the first day of Unleavened Bread, when they sacrificed the Passover lamb, his disciples said to him, 'Where will you have us go and prepare for you to eat the Passover?'"
- **Matt 26:17** — "The disciples came to Jesus, saying, 'Where will you have us prepare for you to eat the Passover?'"
- **Feeding of the 5,000** — Jesus blesses and distributes fish (Mark 6:41; Matt 14:19; Luke 9:16; John 6:11).
- **Feeding of the 4,000** — Again with fish (Mark 8:7; Matt 15:36).
- **Post-resurrection** — Jesus eats broiled fish (Luke 24:42–43) and cooks fish for the disciples (John 21:9–13).

The speaker's own source — the canonical gospels — contains extensive counterevidence to the implied claim that Jesus avoided animal products.

**Verdict:** Stratagem 11 (Induction with Concealed Counterexamples) confirmed.

### 3. Covenantal and Festival Framework

The speaker's thesis requires that the original Passover may not have included lamb. The Torah's covenantal festival framework contradicts this:

- **Exodus 12:3–11** — Detailed instructions for selecting, slaughtering, and eating the Passover lamb. "Your lamb shall be without blemish… the whole assembled congregation of Israel shall slaughter it at twilight" (12:5–6).
- **Exodus 12:8** — "They shall eat the lamb that same night; they shall eat it roasted over the fire with unleavened bread and bitter herbs."
- **Deuteronomy 16:2** — "You shall offer the passover sacrifice to the LORD your God, from the flock and the herd."
- **Leviticus 23:5** — The Passover is integrated into the festival calendar.

These texts are structurally central to the Torah. Any argument about "the original Passover" that does not engage with Exodus 12 directly is incomplete. The speaker never addresses Exodus 12.

**Verdict:** Critical omission of structurally central covenantal material.
