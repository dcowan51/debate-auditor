## SECTION B — Schopenhauer Stratagem Analysis

### Stratagem 11 — Induction with Concealed Counterexamples (PRIMARY)

**Timestamp:** 2:44 and throughout
**Quote:** **"A blood atonement, faith alone. Sorry, none of that exists in the mouth of Jesus."**

This is the load-bearing stratagem of the entire monologue. Abke claims Jesus never taught blood atonement, but the synoptic gospels — the very texts he appeals to as authoritative — contain explicit atonement language from Jesus's mouth: Mark 10:45 ("to give his life as a ransom for many"), Mark 14:24 ("This is my blood of the covenant, which is poured out for many"), and Matthew 26:28 ("which is poured out for many for the forgiveness of sins"). Abke does not address, explain away, or even acknowledge these passages. This is not a fringe proof text — Mark 14:24 is Jesus's own institution of the Eucharist. The concealment of these counterexamples is structurally central to the thesis.

Similarly, Abke claims Jesus never taught judgment or separation (implied at 2:37), while the synoptic gospels contain extensive judgment material: Matthew 25:31-46 (sheep and goats — "Depart from me, you who are cursed, into the eternal fire prepared for the devil and his angels"), Matthew 13:41-42 (weeping and gnashing of teeth), Mark 9:43-48 (Gehenna), Matthew 7:13-14 (narrow gate), and multiple parables of eschatological judgment.

### Stratagem 11 — Induction with Concealed Counterexamples (SECOND INSTANCE — IRONIC)

**Timestamp:** 16:28–16:36
**Quote:** **"Do not resist an evil person, but overcome evil with good. And in doing so, you will heap burning coals upon their head."**

In a monologue arguing that churches substitute Paul for Jesus, Abke attributes Paul's own words to Jesus. "Overcome evil with good" is Romans 12:21 and "heap burning coals upon their head" is Romans 12:20 — both written by Paul, not spoken by Jesus. This is a structurally devastating error: Abke's thesis is that Paul's teachings should be de-emphasized in favor of Jesus's, yet his climactic ethical summary of "what Jesus taught" unwittingly includes Paul's words. The concealed counterexample here is that Paul taught the very love-ethic Abke claims only Jesus taught.

### Source Context Verification

**1. Full-chapter context check — Mark 14:24.**
Mark 14 is the Last Supper narrative. Jesus takes bread, breaks it, takes the cup, and says: "This is my blood of the covenant, which is poured out for many" (Mark 14:24 NRSVUE). Matthew 26:28 adds "for the forgiveness of sins." This is not a stray verse that can be read differently in context — it is Jesus's own eucharistic institution, central to all three synoptic accounts, situated in the Passion narrative. Abke's claim that blood atonement language "does not exist in the mouth of Jesus" requires ignoring one of the most well-attested sayings in the synoptic tradition.

**2. Same-source cross-reference — Synoptic judgment material.**
Abke appeals to the synoptic gospels for his thesis that Jesus taught only love, forgiveness, and present-tense salvation. But these same synoptic gospels contain: Matthew 25:31-46 (sheep and goats — future judgment, "eternal fire," "eternal punishment"), Matthew 13:41-42 (Son of Man sending angels, "furnace of fire"), Mark 9:43-48 (Gehenna, "where their worm does not die and the fire is not quenched"), Matthew 7:13-14 (narrow gate), Matthew 10:28 ("fear the one who can destroy both soul and body in Gehenna"), Luke 16:19-31 (rich man and Lazarus — post-mortem conscious suffering). Abke cannot selectively accept synoptic love-and-forgiveness passages while ignoring structurally central synoptic judgment passages from the same source.

**3. Same-source cross-reference — Mark 10:45.**
"For the Son of Man came not to be served but to serve, and to give his life as a ransom for many." This verse appears in the context of Jesus teaching his disciples about servant leadership. The "ransom" (Greek: λύτρον, lytron) language directly contradicts Abke's claim that Jesus never taught sacrificial/ransoming concepts. The word is a commercial/legal term meaning "price paid for release."

**4. Omission of counterevidence from cited sources — CRITICAL.**
Abke's thesis rests entirely on the synoptic gospels as authoritative sources for what Jesus actually taught. His own cited source contains obvious, extensive counterevidence that he does not acknowledge. This triggers **Stratagem 11 (Induction with Concealed Counterexamples)** at the structural level — the entire argument depends on selective reading of the very texts it claims to honor.

**5. Misattribution check — Romans 12:20-21.**
At 16:28-16:36, Abke presents a stream of Jesus's teachings culminating in "overcome evil with good" and "heap burning coals upon their head." Both phrases are from Romans 12:20-21 — Paul's letter to the Romans (with "burning coals" originally from Proverbs 25:21-22). Neither phrase appears in any Gospel. This misattribution is especially damaging because it demonstrates that Abke cannot cleanly separate "Jesus's teachings" from "Paul's teachings" even in his own presentation — the very distinction his entire thesis depends on.

## SECTION H — Mostly True & Fair Claims

1. **Marcion of Sinope emphasized Paul's letters and was declared a heretic** — Historically well-established. Marcion (c. 85–160 CE) created a Paul-centric canon and was excommunicated c. 144 CE (Tertullian, *Adversus Marcionem*; Wikipedia, "Marcion of Sinope").
2. **The early church's response to Marcion involved developing its own Pauline interpretation** — Scholarly consensus confirms the Marcionite crisis accelerated canonization and Pauline hermeneutics (Harnack, *Marcion: The Gospel of the Alien God*, 1924).
3. **"Faith alone" as a formula does not appear in Jesus's mouth in the synoptic Gospels** — True. The sola fide formulation derives from Paul (Romans 3:28, interpreted through Luther). Jesus uses "your faith has saved you" (Luke 7:50) but not the formal "faith alone" construct.
4. **Richard Rohr is associated with the question "Did Jesus come to change God's mind about us, or our mind about God?"** — Correctly attributed (with appropriate hedging: "I think it's Richard Rohr").
5. **Jesus taught "Repent and God forgives you"** — Supported by multiple synoptic passages: Luke 15 (parables of lost sheep, coin, son), Luke 18:9-14 (Pharisee and tax collector — "this man went home justified").
6. **Jesus taught "Forgive and you'll be forgiven"** — Luke 6:37 ("Forgive, and you will be forgiven"), Matthew 6:14-15 ("If you forgive others their trespasses, your heavenly Father will also forgive you").
