## SECTION B — Schopenhauer Stratagem Analysis

### Stratagem 1 — The Extension (Generalization)
**Timestamp:** 0:33–1:12
**Quote:** **"I'm going to list 20 things that should be objectively wrong according to God... surely if we have 20 things that are objectively wrong according to this Bible according to this God then the number one person who should never break them without excuse would be God himself"**
**Explanation:** The speaker extends the Christian claim about objective morality into a claim that God must personally follow every human moral instruction. This generalizes the theistic position beyond what most theologians actually argue — most distinguish between God's moral commands for creatures and God's sovereign prerogatives as Creator. The extension makes the thesis easier to attack.

### Stratagem 5 — Use Your Opponent's Beliefs Against Him (False Premises)
**Timestamp:** 1:37–1:52
**Quote:** **"I'd like to point you to all of the verses about God's immutability meaning God does not change he is the same yesterday today and forever... if these things were important to God at one point and they were wrong at one point they should be wrong now"**
**Explanation:** The speaker attributes Hebrews 13:8 (about Jesus Christ's nature) to a general principle about God's moral commands never changing. Most Christian theologians distinguish between God's unchanging character and progressive revelation in how moral law is applied across covenants. The speaker uses a premise his audience accepts (immutability) but extends it to territory most theologians would not.

### Stratagem 11 — Induction with Concealed Counterexamples
**Timestamp:** 12:06–12:30
**Quote:** **"God did not forgive at all... Adam and Eve messed up one time before they knew what right or wrong was and he cursed everybody for all time"**
**Explanation:** The speaker builds an inductive case that God is unforgiving by collecting instances of unforgiveness while concealing substantial counterevidence from the same source: the prodigal son parable (Luke 15), God's repeated forgiveness of Israel (Judges cycle), the entire sacrificial system designed for atonement, Psalm 103:12 ("as far as the east is from the west"), Micah 7:18-19, etc. The Bible's dominant narrative arc is precisely about forgiveness and restoration, which the speaker omits.

### Stratagem 12 — Misleading Metaphor / Loaded Language
**Timestamp:** 5:46
**Quote:** **"was to torture and then kill David's infant son for 7 days"**
**Explanation:** The text (2 Samuel 12:15-18) says the child became "very ill" and died on the seventh day. Substituting "torture" for "illness" is a loaded language choice that inflames the audience but is not supported by the source text. This substitution recurs throughout the transcript ("sexual slavery," "sent off to die," "forced cannibalism") where the speaker consistently selects the most inflammatory possible reading.

### Stratagem 24 — Drawing False Consequences
**Timestamp:** 23:28–23:53
**Quote:** **"God accepts of child sacrifice. He gave a victory to this man because of what he was willing to give up which was his daughter."**
**Explanation:** The speaker draws a false consequence from the Jephthah narrative (Judges 11). The text records that Jephthah made a rash vow and God granted victory over the Ammonites — but the text never states God endorsed or accepted the sacrifice itself. The book of Judges consistently presents a moral decline in Israel ("everyone did what was right in his own eyes" — Judges 21:25). Jephthah's act is narrated, not approved. Jeremiah 7:31 and 19:5 explicitly state God never commanded human sacrifice.

### Stratagem 25 — Finding One Instance to the Contrary
**Timestamp:** 20:18–20:43
**Quote:** **"how did we get a change in God's opinion on divorce... why did the law change"**
**Explanation:** The speaker finds one instance where biblical law shifts (divorce standards from Moses to Jesus) and uses it to invalidate the entire concept of objective morality. Jesus himself explains this shift: "Because of your hardness of heart Moses allowed you to divorce... but from the beginning it was not so" (Matthew 19:8). The change is explicitly addressed in the text as a concession, not a contradiction — but the speaker presents it as proof the entire system is subjective.

## SECTION F — Most Likely False or Misleading Claims

### 1. Mayan sacrifice was "most likely a lie perpetuated by missionaries"
**Rating:** Likely false — Strong archaeological evidence confirms Mayan human sacrifice (skeletal remains, DNA evidence from Chichén Itzá, codex depictions). **Confidence: Very High.**
**Evidence:** National Geographic (2024) DNA study of child remains at Chichén Itzá; archaeological record at Palenque, Calakmul.
**What would settle it:** N/A — already settled by archaeology.
**Timestamp + Quote:** 21:29 — **"have you ever heard a Christian talk about the ancient Mayans and how awful it was that they sacrificed so many people even though this was most likely a lie perpetuated by the missionaries who went down there"**

### 2. God "accepted" Jephthah's child sacrifice and "made him go through with it"
**Rating:** Likely false — The text (Judges 11) never states God accepted or endorsed the sacrifice. Judges is a narrative of Israel's moral decline. Jeremiah 7:31, 19:5, 32:35 explicitly deny God ever commanded human sacrifice. **Confidence: High.**
**Evidence:** Jeremiah 7:31; Judges 21:25 (editorial framing of moral decline); scholarly consensus (e.g., Trent Butler, *Judges*, WBC).
**What would settle it:** A passage stating God approved Jephthah's act — none exists.
**Timestamp + Quote:** 23:28 — **"God accepts of child sacrifice he gave a victory to this man because of what he was willing to give up which was his daughter and yes God made him go through with it unlike Abraham"**

### 3. God "tortured" David's infant son for 7 days
**Rating:** Misleading — 2 Samuel 12:15 says the child became "very ill" and died on the seventh day. "Torture" is an emotionally charged substitution not supported by the text. **Confidence: High.**
**Evidence:** 2 Samuel 12:15-18 (NRSVUE).
**What would settle it:** A lexical analysis of the Hebrew; the verb used is *nāgap* (to strike/afflict), not a torture term.
**Timestamp + Quote:** 5:46 — **"was to torture and then kill David's infant son for 7 days"**

### 4. "About 630 laws in the Old Testament"
**Rating:** Mostly false — The established count is 613 mitzvot (Talmud, Makkot 23b). No scholarly source gives 630. **Confidence: Very High.**
**Evidence:** Talmud Makkot 23b; Maimonides' *Sefer HaMitzvot*.
**What would settle it:** Citation of a source using 630 — none found.
**Timestamp + Quote:** 2:24 — **"there's also about 630 laws in the Old Testament"**

### 5. "Only witches — female witches — were killed"
**Rating:** Misleading — Exodus 22:18 uses feminine form, but Leviticus 20:27 prescribes death for both male and female practitioners of sorcery. **Confidence: High.**
**Evidence:** Leviticus 20:27 (NRSVUE): "A man or a woman who is a medium or a wizard shall be put to death."
**What would settle it:** The counter-text already settles it.
**Timestamp + Quote:** 33:49 — **"only witches female witches were killed"**

### 6. Menstruation requires a "sin penance"
**Rating:** Misleading — Leviticus 15:19-30 prescribes a purification offering for ritual cleanness, not moral atonement. The Hebrew *chatat* offering addresses ceremonial impurity, not sin in the moral sense. **Confidence: High.**
**Evidence:** Jacob Milgrom, *Leviticus 1-16* (Anchor Bible): distinguishes purification from sin offerings.
**What would settle it:** Milgrom's analysis is definitive in the field.
**Timestamp + Quote:** 33:56 — **"menstruation is an infirmity that requires a sin penance"**

### 7. Nehemiah 13:23-30 concerns "interracial marriage" as a "great evil"
**Rating:** Misleading — The text's concern is religious syncretism and covenantal identity, not race. Nehemiah objects to marriages with women who worship foreign gods. Ruth (a Moabite) is celebrated precisely because she adopted Yahweh worship. **Confidence: High.**
**Evidence:** Ezra-Nehemiah scholarship (e.g., Tamara Cohn Eskenazi, *JPS Bible Commentary: Ezra-Nehemiah*); the book of Ruth as counter-example.
**What would settle it:** Consistent biblical pattern of accepting converts regardless of ethnicity (Rahab, Ruth, Uriah the Hittite).
**Timestamp + Quote:** 36:30 — **"interracial marriage here is seen as a great evil"**

### 8. Mixed wives and children "sent off essentially to die"
**Rating:** Mostly false — Nehemiah 13 and Ezra 10 describe divorce and sending away, but "sent off to die" is not in the text and is an unsupported inference. **Confidence: High.**
**Evidence:** Ezra 10; Nehemiah 13:25 (harsh treatment directed at men, not mass death of children).
**What would settle it:** Any textual evidence they were killed — none exists.
**Timestamp + Quote:** 37:05 — **"all of the mixed wives and children were sent off essentially to die"**

### 9. Judges 12:4-6: 42,000 killed "because their accent offended"
**Rating:** Misleading — The Ephraimites initiated an armed invasion against Gilead. The accent test (Shibboleth) was a battlefield identification method to catch fleeing enemy soldiers, not a cause of the conflict. **Confidence: High.**
**Evidence:** Judges 12:1-4 (Ephraim's aggression and invasion preceded the Shibboleth test).
**What would settle it:** Reading the passage's full context.
**Timestamp + Quote:** 37:12 — **"the foreigner's accent offends... so much that 42,000 are struck dead"**

### 10. Hebrews 13:8 as proof of God's immutability regarding moral law
**Rating:** Misleading — Hebrews 13:8 ("Jesus Christ is the same yesterday and today and forever") refers to Christ's nature and faithfulness, not to the permanence of every OT legal code. Most theologians distinguish ontological immutability from covenantal progression. **Confidence: High.**
**Evidence:** Hebrews 13:8 text; standard systematic theology (e.g., Wayne Grudem, *Systematic Theology*, ch. 11).
**What would settle it:** Exegetical analysis of Hebrews 13:8 in context — already well-established.
**Timestamp + Quote:** 1:37 — **"God does not change he is the same yesterday today and forever"**
