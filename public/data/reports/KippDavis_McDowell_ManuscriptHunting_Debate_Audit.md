## SECTION F — Most Likely False or Misleading Claims

**1. The SWBTS fragment contained "Leviticus 21 that condemns homosexuality"**
**Rating:** Misleading — High confidence
**Evidence:** The SWBTS fragment contained text from Leviticus 18 and 20, the two chapters containing the Torah's explicit prohibitions on male homosexual intercourse (Lev 18:22; 20:13). Leviticus 21 addresses priestly physical disqualifications and contains no prohibition on homosexuality. The underlying argument (that SWBTS spent heavily on a politically charged anti-homosexuality passage) is correct; only the chapter citation is wrong. [Variant Readings, Brent Nongbri](https://brentnongbri.com/2020/04/08/statement-on-the-so-called-dead-sea-scrolls-of-southwestern-baptist-theological-seminary/); [INTO More](https://www.intomore.com/culture/fake-dead-sea-scrolls-pushed-homophobia/)
**What would settle it:** Consulting any edition of the SWBTS exhibition materials or the Baptist Press press release confirming fragment contents.
**Timestamp + Quote:** 41:26 — **"one of these contained texts from a passage in leviticus 21 that condemns homosexuality"**

---

**2. Patterson "solicited millions to acquire eight fragments" for SWBTS**
**Rating:** Likely false in the fragment count — Medium confidence
**Evidence:** SWBTS's own April 2020 statement describes "six fragments purchased in two separate acquisitions in 2010." Baptist Press reporting mentions eight fragments in negotiation but only six were ultimately purchased. Davis says eight; the institutional record says six.
**What would settle it:** Original sales contracts or SWBTS board minutes confirming the final purchased quantity.
**Timestamp + Quote:** 41:06 — **"solicited millions of dollars from donors to acquire eight fragments"**

---

**3. The supposed first-century Mark fragment was "a 3rd century fragment"**
**Rating:** Misleading by simplification — Medium confidence
**Evidence:** When Papyrus 137 (the Obbink/EES fragment) was published in *The Oxyrhynchus Papyri* vol. LXXXIII (2018), its editors assigned a date of "late 2nd or earlier 3rd century CE." Calling it simply "3rd century" drops the early endpoint and overstates the distance from an embarrassing first-century claim. The correction is directionally correct but imprecise.
**What would settle it:** The published *Oxyrhynchus Papyri* LXXXIII edition, P.Oxy. 5345.
**Timestamp + Quote:** 47:35 — **"actually a 3rd century fragment in the oxyrhynchus papyri collection of the egypt exploration society"**

---

**4. "This was the same fragment that McDowell had claimed to discover in his 2013 talk"**
**Rating:** Potentially misleading — Medium confidence
**Evidence:** Davis implies that the fragment Carroll identified as "Mark 15" during McDowell's December 2013 "Discover the Evidence" mask-dismounting event was identical to P137 (shown by Obbink to Museum of Bible representatives in late 2011 and announced by Wallace in February 2012). This conflation is not established in the video. P137 was from the EES's Oxyrhynchus collection; McDowell's mask fragments were purportedly dismounted from Egyptian cartonnage McDowell owned. Davis provides no direct evidence linking the two, and the provenance chains are distinct.
**What would settle it:** A documented chain of custody connecting the Carroll/McDowell December 2013 Mark fragment to P.Oxy. 5345 / P137.
**Timestamp + Quote:** 43:50 — **"this was the same fragment that mcdowell had claimed to discover in his 2013 talk"**

---

**5. Sean McDowell is "an assistant professor in the Christian apologetics program at Biola University"**
**Rating:** Likely outdated — Low-Medium confidence
**Evidence:** Sean McDowell joined Biola's faculty in 2013. By 2023 Biola's own faculty profile and multiple secondary sources identify him as *Associate* Professor at Talbot School of Theology. "Assistant professor" may have been accurate at the time of production but is imprecise at publication if the video dates to 2020 or later. The distinction matters in an academic context.
**What would settle it:** Confirmed video production date; Biola faculty records for the relevant year.
**Timestamp + Quote:** 1:22 — **"an assistant professor in the christian apologetics program at biola university"**

---

**6. The Baylor 2012 event "was itself a hoax at which no new discoveries were actually made"**
**Rating:** Imprecise characterization — Medium confidence
**Evidence:** The Baylor event was a mask-dismounting exhibition. Describing it as a "hoax" implies intentional deception of the audience, which Davis does not demonstrate. The more accurate characterization in the scholarly record is that no new discoveries were made and the event was theatrically staged. The underlying claim (nothing found) may be correct; the word "hoax" is stronger than the evidence supports.
**What would settle it:** Contemporaneous reporting from the Baylor event; participants' accounts.
**Timestamp + Quote:** 44:10 — **"this particular exhibition it turns out was itself a hoax at which no new discoveries were actually made"**
