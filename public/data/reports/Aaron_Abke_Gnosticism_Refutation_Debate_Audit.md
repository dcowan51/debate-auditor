## SECTION B — Schopenhauer Stratagem Analysis

**Stratagem 11 — Induction with Concealed Counterexamples** (×3 instances)

*Instance 1* — 10:13
**"Gnosticism is founded or was founded upon Paul's teachings through a man named Marcion of Sinope."**
Abke presents Marcion as the origin of gnosticism while concealing that Gnostic movements (Simon Magus, Cerinthus, Saturninus, Basilides, Valentinus) predated or developed independently of Marcion. The leading Marcion scholar, Adolf Harnack, explicitly argued that Marcion was NOT a Gnostic. Abke's entire thesis depends on suppressing this counterevidence.

*Instance 2* — 38:39
**"This is also what essentially Protestantism teaches today — that Jesus is purely divine. He pre-existed with God. He was the Word made flesh."**
Abke equates Protestant Christology with docetism while concealing that every major Protestant confession (Augsburg, Westminster, London Baptist, Belgic) affirms the Chalcedonian Definition: Christ is fully God AND fully human. The pre-existence of Christ and the Word made flesh are not docetism — docetism denies the humanity; Chalcedon affirms both natures. The counterevidence is the entire history of Protestant creedal Christology.

*Instance 3* — 24:45
**"Rather than calling it the demiurge, they call it the devil. But it's the same concept."**
Abke equates the Christian concept of the devil with the Gnostic demiurge while concealing the fundamental structural difference: the demiurge is the CREATOR of the material world in Gnostic cosmology; the devil in Christianity is a fallen creature who creates nothing. The roles are categorically different. Abke suppresses this distinction to make his Protestantism-equals-gnosticism thesis work.

**Stratagem 1 — Extension of the Opponent's Proposition**

— 5:41
**"The Orthodox Catholic Church represents probably the furthest possible deviation from truth that is possible, which is of course the belief, the assertion and emphasis that we are separate from God, which is an ontological impossibility."**
Abke extends Orthodox/Protestant teaching into an ontological claim it does not make. Classical Christian theology (including Eastern Orthodox theosis, Thomistic participation, and even Reformed covenant theology) does not assert ontological separation from God. Total depravity concerns moral inability, not ontological disconnection. Abke extends the opponent's position beyond what it actually asserts.

**Stratagem 8 — Making the Opponent Angry**

This is a deliberately provocative and emotionally charged caricature of penal substitutionary atonement designed to make the doctrine appear absurd and its holders appear foolish. Abke classifies penal substitutionary atonement under the pejorative labels "paganism" and "demonic" to dispose of the argument without engaging its theological defenders (Anselm, Calvin, Hodge, Packer, Schreiner). The labels function as dismissals, not arguments.

## SECTION F — Most Likely False or Misleading Claims

**1. "Gnosticism is founded or was founded upon Paul's teachings through a man named Marcion of Sinope."**
Rating: **Likely false** — reverses the historical chronology. Confidence: High.
Evidence: Proto-Gnostic movements (Simon Magus, Cerinthus, Saturninus) predate Marcion by decades. Basilides and Valentinus developed independently. Harnack (*Marcion: The Gospel of the Alien God*, 1924) explicitly argued Marcion was NOT a Gnostic but a radical biblical theologian. King, *What Is Gnosticism?* (2003) further complicates the category.
What would settle it: A scholarly source establishing Marcion as the chronological origin of Gnostic thought — none exists.
Timestamp — **"gnosticism is founded or was founded upon Paul's teachings through a man named Marcion of Sinope"** — 10:13

**2. "This is also what essentially Protestantism teaches today — that Jesus is purely divine. He pre-existed with God."**
Rating: **Likely false** — contradicts all major Protestant confessions. Confidence: Very High.
Evidence: The Chalcedonian Definition (451 CE), affirmed by all mainstream Protestant traditions, declares Christ "truly God and truly man... in two natures, without confusion, without change, without division, without separation." Westminster Confession (1646), ch. 8.2: "two whole, perfect, and distinct natures, the Godhead and the manhood, were inseparably joined together in one person."
What would settle it: Any major Protestant confession teaching that Jesus had "no human element whatsoever" — none exists.
Timestamp — **"this is also what essentially Protestantism teaches today — that Jesus is purely divine, he pre-existed with God, he was the Word made flesh"** — 38:39

**3. "Rather than calling it the demiurge, they call it the devil. But it's the same concept."**
Rating: **Likely false** — conflates categorically different theological concepts. Confidence: High.
Evidence: The Gnostic demiurge is the CREATOR of the material world — an ignorant or malevolent deity who made physical reality. The Christian devil is a fallen creature (Isa 14:12, Ezek 28:12-19 in traditional reading; Rev 12:9) who created nothing. The demiurge is a cosmic architect; the devil is a rebellious subject. These are structurally incompatible roles. See Layton, *The Gnostic Scriptures* (1987).
What would settle it: A systematic theology equating the two roles — none does.
Timestamp — **"rather than calling it the demiurge, they call it the devil. But it's the same concept"** — 24:45

**4. "In 1 Corinthians Paul says that the Ten Commandments that were etched in stone through Moses are a Ministry of death."**
Rating: **Mostly false** — wrong book; decontextualized. Confidence: Very High.
Evidence: The passage is 2 Corinthians 3:7, not 1 Corinthians. The full context (2 Cor 3:7-11) is a typological comparison: "Now if the ministry of death, chiseled in letters on stone tablets, came in glory... how much more will the ministry of the Spirit come in glory?" Paul is saying the old covenant had glory but the new has greater glory — not that the commandments are evil.
What would settle it: The text itself, which is verifiable.
Timestamp — **"in 1 Corinthians Paul says that the Ten Commandments that were etched in stone through Moses are a Ministry of death"** — 14:38

**5. Paul calls the disciples "so-called Apostles... deceitful workmen, false apostles, messengers of Satan."**
Rating: **Misleading** — conflates unnamed opponents with the Twelve. Confidence: High.
Evidence: 2 Corinthians 11:13-15: "For such boasters are false apostles, deceitful workers, disguising themselves as apostles of Christ." Mainstream scholarship (Furnish, *2 Corinthians*, AB; Barnett, *The Second Epistle to the Corinthians*, NICNT) identifies these as rival missionaries in Corinth, NOT the Twelve. Galatians 2:9 shows Paul affirming James, Cephas, and John as "acknowledged pillars."
What would settle it: Scholarly consensus on the identity of the "super-apostles" — consensus favors rival missionaries, not the Twelve.
Timestamp — **"calling them so-called Apostles... those deceitful workmen, false apostles, messengers of Satan"** — 15:54

**6. Paul told people "if you're a slave, don't worry about getting free."**
Rating: **Likely false** — Paul says the opposite. Confidence: Very High.
Evidence: 1 Corinthians 7:21 (NRSVUE): "Were you a slave when called? Do not be concerned about it. Even if you can gain your freedom, make use of the opportunity." Paul explicitly encourages slaves to gain freedom if possible. Abke reverses Paul's instruction.
What would settle it: The text itself.
Timestamp — **"if you're a slave don't worry about getting free"** — 27:09

**7. "Paul was a lifelong Pharisee steeped in the temple sacrifice system."**
Rating: **Misleading** — conflates Pharisaism with the priestly temple cult. Confidence: High.
Evidence: Pharisees were Torah scholars and synagogue teachers, not temple priests. The temple sacrifice system was administered by the Sadducean priestly caste (Josephus, *Antiquities* 20.199). Paul identifies as a Pharisee (Phil 3:5) but was not "steeped in the temple sacrifice system" — that was the Sadducees' domain. See Sanders, *Judaism: Practice and Belief, 63 BCE–66 CE* (1992).
What would settle it: Any source identifying Pharisees as administrators of the temple sacrifice system — none does.
Timestamp — **"being a lifelong Pharisee steeped in the temple sacrifice system"** — 8:22

**8. "Marcion's dualistic theology and radical reinterpretation of Christian doctrine through Pauline theology greatly influenced later Gnostic thought."**
Rating: **Mostly false** — reverses the chronological relationship. Confidence: High.
Evidence: Major Gnostic systems (Valentinianism, Basilides, Sethianism) developed independently of Marcion. Valentinus was active in Rome c. 136-166 CE, contemporaneous with Marcion; Basilides preceded him (c. 120-140 CE). Harnack argued Marcion influenced the ORTHODOX response to gnosticism, not gnosticism itself. See Pearson, *Gnosticism and Christianity in Roman and Coptic Egypt* (2004).
What would settle it: Evidence of Marcionite ideas entering established Gnostic systems — scholarly evidence runs the other direction.
Timestamp — **"Marcion's dualistic theology and radical reinterpretation of Christian doctrine through Pauline theology greatly influenced later Gnostic thought"** — 10:51

**9. The Pastoral Epistles are "known forgeries to critical scholars that are late 2nd century Pauline forgeries."**
Rating: **Misleading** — overstates dating and terminology. Confidence: High.
Evidence: The majority of critical scholars do reject Pauline authorship of 1-2 Timothy and Titus, but the scholarly term is "pseudepigraphic," not "forgeries" (Ehrman's *Forged* is a minority framing). The consensus dating is early-to-mid 2nd century (c. 80-120 CE), not "late 2nd century." See Johnson, *1 Timothy, 2 Timothy, Titus* (AB); Mounce, *Pastoral Epistles* (WBC).
What would settle it: Standard reference works on Pastoral Epistles dating.
Timestamp — **"these are known forgeries to critical scholars that are late 2nd century... Pauline forgeries"** — 11:55

**10. "Total depravity" means humans are "evil to the core, worthy of nothing but eternal hellfire."**
Rating: **Misleading** — caricatures the doctrine. Confidence: High.
Evidence: Total depravity in Reformed theology means every faculty of human nature is affected by sin — NOT that humans are as evil as possible or possess no goodness. Calvin (*Institutes* 2.2.12-17) explicitly affirms that the image of God remains in fallen humanity and that common grace enables civic virtue. "Worthy of nothing but eternal hellfire" is not how any major Reformed confession defines total depravity.
What would settle it: The actual text of Reformed confessions (Westminster, Canons of Dort, Belgic).
Timestamp — **"we are totally depraved sinners, evil to the core, worthy of nothing but eternal hellfire"** — 5:57

## SECTION H — Mostly True & Fair Claims

1. **Marcion of Sinope (c. 85–160 CE) was an influential early Christian theologian** — Well-established. Eusebius, *Ecclesiastical History* 4.11; Harnack, *Marcion*.
2. **Marcion is widely regarded as the first major Christian heretic** — Standard characterization in patristics and church history (Irenaeus, *Against Heresies* 1.27; Tertullian, *Against Marcion*).
3. **Tertullian's Against Marcion is a five-book work** — Correct. Written c. 207-212 CE; the longest of Tertullian's extant treatises.
4. **Around 140 CE Marcion proposed the first known Christian canon** — Scholarly consensus (Harnack; Gamble, *Books and Readers in Early Christianity*).
5. **Marcion's canon consisted of a redacted Luke and 10 Pauline Epistles** — Correct (Harnack; Metzger, *The Canon of the New Testament*).
6. **Marcion excluded the Pastoral Epistles and Hebrews** — Correct (Tertullian, *Against Marcion* 5.21).
7. **Marcion claimed Paul was the only true Apostle** — Confirmed by Tertullian, Hippolytus, and Epiphanius.
