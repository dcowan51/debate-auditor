## SECTION B — Schopenhauer Stratagem Analysis

### Stratagem 1 — Extension (Amplification of the Opponent's Claim)

**Timestamp:** 21:16–22:03
**Quote:** **"Now to one who works, meaning does good deeds right, loves their neighbor as themselves, their wages are not reckoned as a gift but as something due — meaning like when you love your neighbor as yourself God's not happy and pleased with it, it's like come on bare minimum dude"**
Abke extends Paul's statement about "works" (which in Romans 4 refers to "works of the law" — i.e., Torah observance as a boundary marker for covenant membership) into a claim that Paul despises all good deeds. This extension allows Abke to make Paul's position appear absurd. The New Perspective on Paul (E.P. Sanders, James Dunn, N.T. Wright) has extensively argued that Paul's "works of the law" refers to identity-marking Torah observances, not moral acts of love. Abke ignores this major scholarly framework entirely.

### Stratagem 8 — Making the Opponent Angry

**Timestamp:** 5:35–5:43
**Quote:** **"Christianity comes along preaching a kind of snake oil Gospel of like ah we can help you just say these words and tell us you believe it in your heart and you're saved"**
Characterizing Christianity as "snake oil" is designed to provoke emotional reaction and delegitimize the opposing view through ridicule rather than argument.

### Stratagem 11 — Induction with Concealed Counterexamples (×3)

**Instance 1 — Timestamp:** 2:01–2:22
**Quote:** **"Salvation sōzō is the Greek word it's not about a future being saved in a future afterlife… it's about being saved right here and right now"**
Sōzō is used across a wide semantic range in the NT, including future eschatological salvation (Romans 5:9, 1 Cor 3:15, 1 Peter 1:5). Abke presents only the present-tense usage while concealing the future-tense uses, even in Jesus's own words (Mark 13:13: "the one who endures to the end will be saved" — future passive).

**Instance 2 — Timestamp:** 26:08
**Quote:** **"Four times in the New Testament once in Matthew and thrice in Revelation Jesus is quoted as saying you will be judged according to your works"**
True that Matthew 16:27 and Revelation 2:23, 20:12-13 contain this language. But Abke conceals that Revelation also says "Blessed are those who wash their robes" (Rev 22:14 NRSVUE) and that the Lamb's Book of Life — not works alone — determines final destiny (Rev 20:15). He selectively quotes the works-judgment texts while omitting the grace/faith texts from the same books.

**Instance 3 — Timestamp:** 30:34–31:12
**Quote:** **"Ebia says very explicitly Jesus gave no command of animal sacrifice circumcision or any of the other precepts of Moses except only the spiritual laws"**
Abke treats the Ebionite Gospel (preserved in Epiphanius, Panarion 30.16.4-5) as authoritative testimony about the historical Jesus while concealing that (a) the Ebionites were a sectarian group considered heretical by virtually all early church traditions — including Jewish Christians who did practice sacrifice; (b) this gospel survives only in fragmentary hostile quotation by Epiphanius; (c) mainstream historical scholarship treats these texts as reflecting Ebionite theology, not Jesus's actual words.

### Stratagem 12 — Choosing Favorable Metaphors

**Timestamp:** 14:04–14:39
**Quote:** **"What good would it do you to go around telling everybody I'm a master violinist no one can play the violin like me… oh no no no I'm a violinist apart from works"**
The violinist analogy replaces the theological category of "faith" (pistis — trust, reliance, faithfulness to God) with a skill-claim, which structurally changes the argument. Skill is demonstrated by performance; trust in a person is a different category. The analogy is rhetorically effective but philosophically misleading.

### Stratagem 25 — Instance to the Contrary (Single Counterexample to Overthrow a Universal)

**Timestamp:** 5:01–5:19
**Quote:** **"I grew up in church seeing lots and lots of people scores of people come to our church cry confess repent say Jesus is their lord… and they never come back again"**
Abke uses anecdotal pastoral failure as a counterexample to overthrow the entire doctrine of justification by faith. The existence of insincere converts does not logically invalidate the doctrine; most Protestant theologians (including Calvin) explicitly address this through the doctrine of perseverance — those who fall away never had genuine faith.

## SECTION F — Most Likely False or Misleading Claims

**1. Martin Luther changed to "double justification" at the end of his life**
Rating: **Likely false** — No credible Luther scholarship supports this claim. Confidence: High.
Evidence: Luther's final writings (including the 1545 autobiographical preface) reaffirm sola fide. "Double justification" was a compromise formula proposed at the Regensburg Colloquy (1541) that both Luther and Rome rejected. Luther never adopted it. ([Christian Study Library](https://www.christianstudylibrary.org/article/martin-luther-and-doctrine-justification-faith-alone); [Wikipedia: Theology of Martin Luther](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theology_of_Martin_Luther))
What would settle it: A citation from Luther's late writings endorsing double justification.
**Timestamp + quote:** 18:14–18:56 — **"Martin Luther at the very end of his life the final years of his life he woke up to this fallacy and he changed his Doctrine to something called double justification"**

**2. John Calvin hated the Book of James and wished it excluded from the canon**
Rating: **Likely false** — Calvin accepted James as canonical and wrote a commentary on it. Confidence: High.
Evidence: Calvin's Commentary on the Catholic Epistles includes a full commentary on James affirming its apostolic authority. Calvin argued James does not contradict Paul but addresses different concerns. ([Called to Communion](https://www.calledtocommunion.com/2019/09/john-calvin-on-dead-faith/); [CCEL: Calvin's Commentaries](https://ccel.org/ccel/calvin/calcom45/calcom45.ii.html))
What would settle it: A citation from Calvin rejecting or wishing to exclude James.
**Timestamp + quote:** 17:40–18:06 — **"John Calvin… they both hated the Book of James… as did John Calvin because these guys were hyper grace teachers"**

**3. Paul was "a serial killer of Nazarene Christians"**
Rating: **Misleading** — "Serial killer" is a modern forensic term implying repetitive individual homicide. Acts describes Paul approving of Stephen's execution (Acts 8:1), casting votes against Christians facing death (Acts 26:10), and imprisoning believers — not personally committing serial murder. Confidence: High.
Evidence: Acts 8:1-3, 9:1-2, 22:4-5, 26:9-11; Galatians 1:13. Bart Ehrman notes Paul was "a persecutor" who used violent measures but the exact nature and extent is debated. ([Bart Ehrman Blog](https://ehrmanblog.org/how-paul-persecuted-the-christians/))
What would settle it: A source describing Paul personally killing multiple people.
**Timestamp + quote:** 44:21 — **"was a serial killer of Nazarene Christians"**

**4. "Here O Israel the Lord your God is one" attributed to Isaiah 45:5**
Rating: **Likely false** — This is the Shema from Deuteronomy 6:4, not Isaiah 45:5. Confidence: Very High.
Evidence: Deuteronomy 6:4 (NRSVUE): "Hear, O Israel: The LORD is our God, the LORD alone." Isaiah 45:5 reads: "I am the LORD, and there is no other; besides me there is no god." Different verse, different book.
What would settle it: N/A — this is a straightforward textual misattribution.

The verse says Paul's writings contain things "hard to understand" (dysnoēta) which the ignorant and unstable twist — implying the content is valid but complex, not that it is nonsensical. ## SECTION H — Mostly True & Fair Claims

1. **Martin Luther called James an "epistle of straw"** — True. Luther used this phrase in his 1522 preface to the NT, though he removed it from later editions. ([Gospel Coalition](https://www.thegospelcoalition.org/themelios/article/the-epistle-of-straw-reflections-on-luther-and-the-epistle-of-james/))
2. **"Many sweat hard at reconciling James with Paul but unsuccessfully"** — True as a Luther quotation. This is a legitimate paraphrase of Luther's own words about the James-Paul tension.
3. **Thomas Aquinas taught that God is pure actuality (actus purus) with no potentiality** — True. This is a foundational principle of Thomistic metaphysics. ([Catholic Encyclopedia](https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/01125b.htm); [IEP: Aquinas Metaphysics](https://iep.utm.edu/thomas-aquinas-metaphysics/))
4. **Aquinas developed act/potency based on Aristotle's metaphysical framework** — True. Aquinas explicitly built on Aristotle's Metaphysics.
5. **"One potential cannot create change in another potential"** — True as a statement of Thomistic metaphysics. Only something in act can reduce a potency to act.
6. **James 2:17 says "Faith by itself, if it does not have works, is dead"** — True. Accurate quotation (NRSVUE).
7. **James 2:24: "You see that a person is justified by works and not by faith alone"** — True. Accurate quotation.
8. **John 8:34: Jesus said "everyone who commits sin is a slave to sin"** — True (content correct, though Abke cited the wrong verse number).
9. **Isaiah 45:5 says "beside me there is none else"** — True. The content of the Isaiah verse is accurate, though Abke conflated it with Deuteronomy 6:4.
10. **2 Corinthians 3:7: Paul refers to the law on stone tablets as a "ministry of death"** — True. Paul writes: "Now if the ministry of death, chiseled in letters on stone tablets, came in glory…" (NRSVUE).
11. **Matthew 16:27 and Revelation passages reference judgment "according to works"** — True. Matt 16:27, Rev 2:23, Rev 20:12-13 all contain this language. The count of "four times" is approximately correct (Matt 16:27, Rev 2:23, Rev 20:12, Rev 20:13).
12. **Paul in 2 Corinthians 11:23 said "I am a better [servant of Christ]"** — True. 2 Cor 11:23 (NRSVUE): "Are they ministers of Christ? I am talking like a madman — I am a better one."
13. **Paul in Galatians 1:20 says "before God, I do not lie"** — True. Gal 1:20 (NRSVUE): "In what I am writing to you, before God, I do not lie!"
14. **Jesus said "this is how they will know you are my disciples, by your love for one another"** — True. John 13:35.
15. **Jesus said "if you love me, keep my commandments"** — True. John 14:15.
16. **2 Peter 3:16 describes Paul's writings as containing things "hard to understand"** — True. The core claim is correct, though the gloss of "nonsense/unintelligible" is not.
17. **Majority of critical scholars consider 2 Peter pseudepigraphal** — True. This is the dominant position in critical NT scholarship.
