## SECTION B — Schopenhauer Stratagem Analysis

### Stratagem 11 — Induction with Concealed Counterexamples

**Timestamp:** 3:22–3:57 / 4:27–5:01
**Quote:** **"Do not eat flesh… How Jesus celebrated Passover is by eating unleavened bread, flat bread without yeast, and gathering around with your family."**

The speaker appeals to the Torah's Passover narrative (Exodus) to explain the unleavened bread tradition while entirely omitting that the same Torah text — Exodus 12:3–8 — commands the slaughtering and eating of a lamb as the central act of Passover. Deuteronomy 16:2 reiterates: "You shall offer the passover sacrifice to the LORD your God, from the flock and the herd." The Synoptic Gospels (Mark 14:12; Luke 22:7–8) explicitly describe preparations to sacrifice and eat the Passover lamb. This is a textbook case of citing a source while concealing structurally central counterevidence from that same source. This is the most consequential stratagem in the sermon because the omission of the Passover lamb is load-bearing for the speaker's vegetarian-Jesus thesis.

### Stratagem 11 — Induction with Concealed Counterexamples (second instance)

**Timestamp:** 12:49
**Quote:** **"Jesus never called himself God. He never called himself the son of God."**

The speaker frames Jesus's self-understanding using only the "son of man" designation while concealing that in Mark 14:61–62, when the high priest asks "Are you the Messiah, the Son of the Blessed One?", Jesus responds "I am" (ἐγώ εἰμι). Jesus also repeatedly refers to God as "my Father" (Matthew 7:21, 10:32–33, 11:27, etc.), which functionally operates as a son-of-God claim. The speaker omits all of this from the same canonical Gospels he is otherwise citing.

### Stratagem 32 — Labeling to Dismiss

**Timestamp:** 2:22, 6:43, 14:36
**Quotes:** **"the Roman pagan Catholic Church"** / **"the Roman pagan cult"** / **"the concoction meth message… of the Roman pagan Catholic Church and Christians"**

Rather than engaging with the historical arguments of proto-orthodox Christianity or patristic scholarship, the speaker applies pejorative labels ("pagan," "cult," "meth message") to dismiss the entire tradition. This functions as a rhetorical substitute for evidence-based rebuttal.

### Stratagem 1 — Extension / Generalization

**Timestamp:** 3:13
**Quote:** **"Jesus never followed the doctrinary legalistic laws and ways of the Pharisees."**

Jesus's well-documented disputes with Pharisees on specific halakhic points (Sabbath observance, purity laws, hand-washing) are extended into a blanket claim that he "never followed" any Pharisaic practices. In fact, Jesus attended synagogue (Luke 4:16), observed festivals, taught in the Temple, and shared core theological commitments with Pharisaism (resurrection of the dead, prophetic authority, monotheism). The generalization distorts a nuanced historical relationship.

### Stratagem 5 — False Premises

**Timestamp:** 3:22–3:26
**Quote:** **"Do not eat flesh. And observe the Sabbath. Observe the same exact holidays that Jesus observed."**

The speaker presents Jesus's vegetarianism as established fact and builds his Passover theology upon it. However, no canonical gospel attributes a vegetarian teaching to Jesus. The canonical evidence includes Jesus eating fish (Luke 24:42–43), multiplying fish for thousands (Mark 6:41, 8:7; Matt 14:19, 15:36; Luke 9:16; John 6:11), and preparing a Passover meal that, per Torah and Synoptic context, included lamb. The vegetarian premise comes from the Gospel of the Ebionites (2nd century, fragmentary), not from Jesus's own attested words. Building the sermon's ethical framework on an unsubstantiated premise qualifies as Stratagem 5.

## SECTION F — Most Likely False or Misleading Claims

**1. Jesus never called himself the son of God.**
Rating: **Likely false** — contradicted by Mark 14:61–62 where Jesus affirms the title before the Sanhedrin. Confidence: High.
Evidence: Mark 14:61–62 (NRSVUE); Bock, *Blasphemy and Exaltation in Judaism* (Mohr Siebeck, 1998).
What would settle it: Comparison of Synoptic trial narratives (Mark 14:61–62, Matt 26:63–64, Luke 22:67–70).
Timestamp: 12:49 — **"He never called himself the son of God."**

**2. Jesus celebrated Passover by eating only unleavened bread (no lamb).**
Rating: **Misleading** — omits the Passover lamb commanded in Exodus 12:3–8 and Deuteronomy 16:2, and the Synoptic Gospels' explicit references to preparing the Passover lamb (Mark 14:12, Luke 22:7–8). Confidence: High.
Evidence: Exodus 12:3–8; Deuteronomy 16:2; Mark 14:12; Luke 22:7–8 (NRSVUE).
What would settle it: Full reading of Exodus 12 alongside the Synoptic Last Supper accounts.
Timestamp: 3:50 — **"How Jesus celebrated Passover is by eating unleavened bread, flat bread without yeast, and gathering around with your family."**

**3. Jesus taught his followers not to eat flesh.**
Rating: **Likely false** — no canonical gospel records this teaching. Canonical evidence includes Jesus eating fish (Luke 24:42–43), multiplying fish (Mark 6:41, 8:7), and preparing Passover (which per Torah included lamb). The vegetarian-Jesus tradition derives from the 2nd-century Gospel of the Ebionites, not from Jesus's attested words. Confidence: High.
Evidence: Luke 24:42–43; Mark 6:41; Mark 8:7; Gospel of the Ebionites fragments (Epiphanius, *Panarion* 30.13).
What would settle it: Survey of all canonical and early non-canonical dietary references attributed to Jesus.
Timestamp: 3:22 — **"Do not eat flesh."**

**4. "Benadam" is the Aramaic term for "son of man."**
Rating: **Mostly false** — "Ben Adam" (בן אדם) is Hebrew. The Aramaic equivalent is "bar enash" (בר אנש) or "bar nasha" (בר נשא), as attested in Daniel 7:13 (Biblical Aramaic: כבר אנש). The speaker conflates Hebrew and Aramaic. Confidence: High.
Evidence: Daniel 7:13 (Aramaic); BDAG and Jastrow lexicons; Hurtado, "The Son of Man: Some Linguistic Observations" (2011).
What would settle it: Lexicographic check of "son of man" in Hebrew vs. Aramaic.
Timestamp: 12:49 — **"He called himself the son of man, Benadam in Aramaic."**

**5. The Ebionites were Jesus's first group / original movement.**
Rating: **Misleading** — the Ebionites are first attested in 2nd-century sources (Irenaeus, *Adversus Haereses* ~180 CE). Whether they represent direct continuity with the Jerusalem church is a matter of scholarly debate, not established fact. The speaker presents this as certain. Confidence: Medium.
Evidence: Irenaeus, *Adversus Haereses* 1.26.2; Klijn & Reinink, *Patristic Evidence for Jewish-Christian Sects* (Brill, 1973).
What would settle it: Review of the full patristic and archaeological evidence for Ebionite origins.
Timestamp: 1:32 — **"We are restoring the original first sect and movement that Jesus started, the sect of the Nazarenes. And later they became more broadly known as the Ebianim."**

**6. The Gospel of the Hebrews is "otherwise known as the Hebrew gospel of Matthew."**
Rating: **Misleading** — modern scholarship distinguishes at least three Jewish-Christian gospels: the Gospel of the Hebrews, the Gospel of the Nazarenes, and the Gospel of the Ebionites. The relationship between any of these and canonical Matthew is debated. The equation is an oversimplification that collapses critical distinctions. Confidence: Medium.
Evidence: Vielhauer & Strecker, in Schneemelcher, *New Testament Apocrypha* vol. 1 (rev. ed., 1991); Craig A. Evans (2005) on the three distinct Jewish gospels.
What would settle it: Comparative analysis of the surviving fragments against canonical Matthew.
Timestamp: 6:15 — **"In the gospel of the Hebrews, otherwise known as the Hebrew gospel of Matthew."**

**7. Modern Judaism was "concocted by the rabbinic movement in 300 CE."**
Rating: **Misleading** — rabbinic Judaism developed over centuries (Mishnah compiled ~200 CE, Jerusalem Talmud ~400 CE, Babylonian Talmud ~500 CE). The dating to "300 CE" is imprecise, and the word "concocted" implies deliberate fabrication rather than organic religious evolution. Confidence: High.
Evidence: Neusner, *From Politics to Piety: The Emergence of Pharisaic Judaism* (1973); Schiffman, *From Text to Tradition* (Ktav, 1991).
What would settle it: Timeline of rabbinic literature and institutional development.
Timestamp: 3:05 — **"the modern idea of Judaism today was actually concocted by the rabbinic movement in 300 CE"**

**8. Jesus never followed the "doctrinary legalistic laws and ways of the Pharisees."**
Rating: **Misleading** — Jesus shared key Pharisaic commitments (resurrection, monotheism, prophetic authority, synagogue worship) while disputing specific halakhic applications. The blanket "never followed" is a generalization unsupported by the Gospel evidence. Confidence: Medium.
Evidence: Sanders, *Jesus and Judaism* (Fortress, 1985); Vermes, *Jesus the Jew* (Fortress, 1973).
What would settle it: Systematic comparison of Jesus's halakhic positions with known Pharisaic teaching.
Timestamp: 3:13 — **"Jesus never followed the doctrinary legalistic laws and ways of the Pharisees."**

## SECTION H — Mostly True & Fair Claims

1. **[2/4] The figure "Ebian" as a founder never existed; scholarly consensus confirms this.** — Tertullian and pseudo-Tertullian invented the figure; Origen correctly identified the Hebrew etymology (evyon = poor). (Ebionites, *Wikipedia*; Klijn & Reinink, 1973.)
2. **[6] Jesus was not a "Christian."** — The term Χριστιανός first appears in Acts 11:26 at Antioch, after Jesus's death. Technically correct and uncontroversial.
3. **[7] Jesus's original disciples were not Christians.** — Same basis as above; the designation postdates the movement's founding.
4. **[8] Jesus's followers internally called themselves "people of the way."** — Attested in Acts 9:2, 19:9, 19:23, 22:4, 24:14, 24:22.
5. **[9] Externally, they were known as the sect of the Nazarenes.** — Acts 24:5 (Tertullus: "a ringleader of the sect of the Nazarenes").
6. **[21] Jesus never explicitly called himself God (in the Synoptic Gospels).** — Mainstream critical scholarship (e.g., Ehrman, Dunn) holds that explicit ontological God-claims are absent from the Synoptics. John's Gospel presents higher Christological claims, but these are widely regarded as later theological reflection.
7. **[23] Jesus referred to himself as the "son of man."** — Attested across all four canonical Gospels. The most frequently used self-designation in the Synoptics (over 80 occurrences).
8. **[25] Jesus preached monotheism / following the one true God.** — Mark 12:29 ("Hear, O Israel: the Lord our God, the Lord is one"). Uncontested.
9. **[27] "The last will be first and the first will be last."** — Direct quotation from Matthew 20:16 (NRSVUE). Accurately cited.
10. **The Parable of the Laborers in the Vineyard is in Matthew 20.** — Correctly located (Matthew 20:1–16).
11. **The parable involves a landowner hiring workers at different hours and paying all the same wage.** — Accurate summary of Matthew 20:1–16.
12. **Unleavened bread during Passover commemorates the Israelites' hasty departure from Egypt.** — Exodus 12:39; standard explanation.
13. **The Ebionites are connected to the Hebrew word for "poor" (evyon).** — Scholarly consensus; confirmed by Origen (*Contra Celsum* 2.1) and modern scholarship.
