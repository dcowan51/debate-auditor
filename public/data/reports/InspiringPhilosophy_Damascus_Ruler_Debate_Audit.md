## SECTION B — Schopenhauer Stratagem Analysis

### Stratagem 1 — Appeal to Eyewitness Authority (MODERATE)
**Timestamp:** 7:21–7:28
**Quote:** **"Paul is a more reliable witness to what was going on in Damascus at the time, especially since he was an eyewitness."**
Assessment: Legitimate rhetorical point but creates asymmetry: Paul's account is primary source, yet speaker doesn't acknowledge that Paul may have been mistaken OR that eyewitness accounts can be unreliable. Conflates "eyewitness" with "necessarily accurate." Weasel factor: Moderate. Sidesteps that eyewitnesses to events can misinterpret or misrepresent political arrangements.

### Stratagem 2 — Argument from Ignorance (MODERATE)
**Timestamp:** 7:08–7:15
**Quote:** **"Josephus never says Aretas didn't gain control... or that Damascus was always under Roman governor. He was also writing long after... we simply do not know enough."**
Assessment: Shifts burden of proof to skeptics: "absence of contradiction = plausibility." This is weak logic—Josephus' silence does not confirm alternative explanations. Weasel factor: Moderate-High. Uses "we don't know" to justify accepting an otherwise unsupported claim.

### Stratagem 3 — Presenting Multiple Options as Equally Valid (MODERATE)
**Timestamp:** 6:47–6:56
**Quote:** **"Any of these three explanations are plausible. We simply do not know enough to assume Paul made a mistake."**
Assessment: Lists three theories with varying degrees of scholarly support (Campbell, Taylor, ethnarch-only) but presents them as equally defensible without ranking by evidence or consensus. Weasel factor: Moderate. Conflates "possible" with "equally probable."

### Stratagem 4 — Suppression of Contrary Evidence (MODERATE-HIGH)
**Timestamp:** 6:47–6:56
**Quote:** Does not mention that mainstream scholarship remains skeptical that Aretas controlled Damascus proper, viewing the ethnarch theory as more plausible than full territorial control.
Assessment: Emphasizes Campbell's bold reconstruction without adequate qualification of its minority status. Weasel factor: High. Campbell's theory, while argued in peer-reviewed work, is not scholarly consensus and receives significant skeptical response.

### Stratagem 5 — Loaded Framing ("Oversimplification")
**Timestamp:** 1:01–1:08
**Quote:** **"This objection is an oversimplification of the complicated political landscape."**
Assessment: Dismisses the skeptical position as naïve without engaging its substantive merits. Presumes complexity favors the speaker's interpretation. Weasel factor: Moderate. Common rhetorical move; structurally sound but used to evade scrutiny.

### Weasel-Word Flags

1. **"plausible"** — Repeated throughout (1:38, 6:36, 6:47) — Used 8+ times without quantifying probability or comparing to null hypothesis. Conflates "not impossible" with "likely."
2. **"may have"** — (3:46, 4:03, 4:12, 6:20) — Appropriate conditional language in some contexts, but overused to introduce speculative claims as legitimate alternatives.
3. **"entirely plausible"** — (6:36, Campbell quote) — Hedged language used to present minority scholarly view as mainstream.
4. **"simply do not know"** — (6:56, 6:56) — Argument from ignorance marker; absence of contradiction ≠ support for theory.
5. **"we can observe"** — (3:13, 3:04) — Suggestive phrasing that presumes causation (chaos → control) without demonstration.
6. **"rightful Nabataean land"** — (6:12, 6:20) — Presentist language projecting modern concepts of territorial "ownership" onto ancient political dynamics.
