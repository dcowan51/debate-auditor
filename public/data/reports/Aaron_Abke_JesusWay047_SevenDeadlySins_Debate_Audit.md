**Source Context Verification:**

1. **Hosea 6:6 full-chapter check:** Hosea 6 opens with Israel's shallow repentance (vv. 1-3): "Come, let us return to the LORD…after two days he will revive us." God responds: "What shall I do with you, O Ephraim? Your love is like a morning cloud" (v. 4). Verse 6 follows: "For I desire steadfast love (*hesed*) and not sacrifice, the knowledge of God rather than burnt offerings." The chapter's subject is Israel's *covenantal fickleness*, not the mechanics of sacrifice. Hosea 8:13 and 9:4 describe Israel's sacrifices as acceptable in principle but ineffective due to sin, implying proper sacrifice has value when covenant faithfulness accompanies it. The dominant scholarly reading (Wolff, Mays, Andersen & Freedman): prioritization of covenant love over ritual compliance — not abolition of the sacrificial system.

**7. The Hebrew Gospel of Matthew is the earliest gospel account**

**Rating:** Misleading — mainstream scholarship holds Mark as the earliest canonical gospel. The "Hebrew Matthew" referenced by Papias (c. 130 CE) is a disputed tradition not identified with the canonical Greek Matthew. Confidence: **High.**

**Evidence:** The two-source hypothesis, supported by the vast majority of New Testament scholars, identifies Mark as the earliest gospel (c. 65-70 CE) with Matthew and Luke drawing on Mark and Q.
